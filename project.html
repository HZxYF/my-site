Five things I might build

Ideas are cheap; these are the five that survived being asked two questions — who is it for, and what are its four pages? The last one is the one I would actually start on Monday.

  1. Campus Bus, Live

    For first-year students who think the walk to the bus stop is five minutes

    One page that answers a single question in under two seconds: when is the next bus, where do I stand, and is it late. No login, no app, no map that needs a mobile connection to draw itself.

    Four pages: now, timetable, alerts, about

  2. Cheap Eats

    For anyone living on a student budget within walking distance of the campus

    A guide to what you can actually eat for under 20 yuan, sorted by how far you have to walk, with a note on whether it is open at nine at night. Plain text and a map, nothing that needs an account.

    Four pages: list, by price, by distance, about

  3. Cantonese Flashcards

    For exchange students who can hear a tone but cannot produce one

    Two hundred phrases with audio, shown one at a time, with a simple right/wrong counter kept in the browser. The point is repetition, not features — so it works offline once the page has loaded.

    Four pages: practise, all phrases, progress, about

  4. Sunday League Stats

    For the five-a-side team that argues about who scored

    A table of matches, goals and assists that one person can update after each game in about a minute. Everything is typed in by hand, which is the point: no accounts, no app, no arguing about the app.

    Four pages: table, matches, players, about

  5. Reading List

    For computer science students with thirty PDFs and no order to them start here

    A page that keeps a list of what to read next, why it is on the list and whether it was worth it. Stored in the browser, exportable as a text file — small enough to finish in a weekend, useful enough to keep using.

    Four pages: now reading, queue, finished, about

Each idea is judged on the same two questions from the course: who is it for, and what are its four pages. If either answer is vague, the idea is not ready.
